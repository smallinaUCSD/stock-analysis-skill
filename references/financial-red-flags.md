# Financial red flags

Quality screen to run alongside valuation. None is disqualifying alone; a
cluster is. Where a number is involved, compute it — don't eyeball.

## Cash flow vs earnings
- Net income rising while operating cash flow stagnates or falls → earnings
  quality problem (aggressive revenue recognition, ballooning receivables).
- Free cash flow persistently negative for a supposedly mature company.
- Large, recurring gaps between GAAP and "adjusted" numbers.

## Balance sheet
- Rising net debt / EBITDA, especially into a slowing business.
- Interest coverage (EBIT / interest) trending toward and below ~3x.
- Goodwill a large share of assets → past acquisitions that may impair.

## Dilution & capital returns
- Share count creeping up year after year (SBC-funded) — "adjusted" EPS hides
  it; look at diluted shares outstanding over time.
- Buybacks executed at obviously rich valuations (destroys value).

## Growth durability
- Reverse-DCF implied growth far above the company's own history or TAM math →
  priced for perfection; small disappointments re-rate hard.
- Customer/revenue concentration; a single product or platform dependency.

## Governance / accounting
- Frequent restatements, auditor changes, or late filings.
- Related-party transactions; unusual insider selling clusters.
- Complex or opaque segment reporting that resists a clean DCF.

## For leveraged/ETN holdings
- Issuer credit risk (ETNs are unsecured) and call/acceleration terms.
- Persistent tracking gap vs. the stated multiple beyond expected decay.
